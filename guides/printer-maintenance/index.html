Printer maintenance: how to keep an inkjet printing

Guide3 min readBy the Ink Comparison teamUpdated 24 Nov 2026

Inkjet printers have a reputation for dying just when you need them, usually the night before a form is due. Most of that comes down to ink drying in the nozzles while the printer sits unused. A few easy habits avoid most of it and save ink as well.

Print something every week

This is the big one. HP recommends using your printer at least once a week, and printing at least a page every one to two weeks to keep installed cartridges healthy. Make it a page with some colour on it, so every nozzle gets a run. If you print rarely, a weekly test page or nozzle check costs far less in ink than a clogged printhead.

Switch off with the power button

Turn the printer off with its own power button, not at the wall or a switched extension lead. HP explains that the printer then parks the printhead and caps it, which stops the ink drying out. If you cut the power, the head can be left uncovered. Wait until the printer has finished whirring before unplugging it.

Leave the cartridges in

Don't take cartridges out to "keep them fresh". Leaving them installed is what HP recommends, and Epson printers need every cartridge in place to work. When one runs out, replace it promptly instead of leaving an empty slot.

Look after the paper path

  • Use paper that suits the printer, and fan the stack before loading it, so sheets don't stick together.
  • Don't overfill the tray, and keep paper flat and dry. Damp, curled paper is a common cause of jams.
  • If pages feed crookedly or not at all, the rubber rollers may be dusty. Many printers have a roller-cleaning option in the Maintenance menu. Otherwise, wipe them gently with a lint-free cloth barely dampened with water, following your manual.
  • Clear jams by pulling paper out slowly in the direction it normally travels, so it doesn't tear and leave scraps inside.

Keep it clean and in the right place

  • Keep the printer somewhere dust-free and away from radiators and sunny windows. Heat dries ink faster.
  • Close the lid and paper trays when you're not using it, and dust the outside now and then. See Printer cleaning: the dos and don'ts for what's safe to use.

Use the maintenance menu, but sparingly

  • Nozzle check: prints a test pattern. It's the quickest way to see if anything's blocked, and it uses very little ink.
  • Head cleaning: only when the nozzle check shows gaps. Each clean uses ink, so don't run it out of habit.
  • Printhead alignment: worth running if lines look jagged or text looks slightly doubled, and after fitting a new printhead.

Going away, or not printing for a while?

Before a long break, print a colourful page, then switch off with the power button and leave the cartridges in. When you come back, print a nozzle check before anything important. If it's patchy, see How to unclog a badly clogged printer. If you know the printer will sit idle for months at a time, an ink tank printer or a laser printer may suit you better. Laser toner doesn't dry out.

Firmware updates and spare cartridges

Two last things. Updates can fix problems, but they can also stop compatible cartridges working, so it's worth choosing when to install them. See How to stop firmware updates blocking your cartridges. And if you keep spare cartridges, store them properly. See How long do ink cartridges last?
