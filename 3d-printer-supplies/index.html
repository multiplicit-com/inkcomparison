3D printer filament and resin

402 3D printer supply prices, checked 27 Nov 2026, 12:34

A 3D printer is cheap to run compared with an inkjet, but the material is the running cost. Most home 3D printers melt plastic filament from a spool. Resin printers cure liquid resin with light instead. Buy the type your printer takes, in the right diameter, and compare spools by price per kilogram rather than price per spool.

Which filament to buy

PLA suits most people: it's the easiest to print and works on almost every printer. Move to PETG for tougher or outdoor parts, and to the others only when a job needs them.

PETGTougher parts, brackets, things used outdoors or near waterEasyStronger and more heat resistant than PLA, but strings more. Needs a hotter nozzle and bed.

Check your printer's maximum nozzle and bed temperatures before buying anything other than PLA or PETG.

Diameter, spool size and price per kilogram

Filament comes in two diameters, and your printer only takes one. 1.75mm is by far the most common. 2.85mm, often sold as 3mm, is used by some Ultimaker and older printers. Your printer's manual or spec sheet says which.

The standard spool is 1kg, but you'll also see 250g and 500g sample spools and 2kg to 5kg bulk spools. A cheap small spool can cost more per kilogram than a dearer big one, so we show the price per kilogram for every listing to make spools easy to compare.

Some printers with automatic multi-material systems don't get on with cardboard spools or very large spools. If yours has one, check what it takes before you buy in bulk.

Resin for resin printers

Resin printers (often called MSLA or SLA printers) build very fine detail, which makes them popular for miniatures, jewellery models and dental work. They use liquid resin that hardens under UV light, and can't use filament.

  • Standard resin is the cheapest and fine for display models, but it can be brittle.
  • Tough or ABS-like resin bends a little before it breaks, so it suits parts that get handled.
  • Water-washable resin can be cleaned with water instead of isopropyl alcohol, which is handier at home.

Most home resin printers use 405nm light, and most resins are made for it, but check the bottle says it suits your printer. Resin is sold by the bottle, usually 500g or 1kg, so compare by price per kilogram as with filament.

Uncured resin irritates skin. Wear nitrile gloves, print in a ventilated room, and keep it away from children and pets.

Keeping filament dry

Most filaments slowly take in moisture from the air. Damp filament pops and strings as it prints and makes weak, rough parts. Keep spools you're not using sealed in a bag with a desiccant sachet. Nylon, TPU and PETG suffer most, and a filament dryer can bring a damp spool back.
